Method

Two series live here. One is measured: prices collected in South Carolina stores every week. The other is a reconstruction, built only from published federal data, that covers the years before collection started. They are never mixed, and every page says which one you are looking at.

The measured series

What gets priced

A fixed list of items, each standing for a category of grocery spending. Every item is priced at its store-brand or unbranded version for the headline basket; where a national brand exists, it is priced too, which is what makes the brand-gap comparison possible. Quantities come from the USDA Thrifty Food Plan (see Basket).

Collecting

Prices come from retailer APIs, one store at a time, on a weekly schedule (Tuesdays, overnight). A collection week is the Monday of that week. Each observation records the price, the pack size as observed and the unit price it implies — pack size is never assumed, because package sizes shrink. Shelf prices and promotional prices are stored separately: the headline uses shelf prices only, and a second series, promo-inclusive, uses the lowest price a shopper could have paid.

Observations are append-only. A re-collection adds rows; it never edits them. The effective price for a week is the latest row for that store, item and price type.

From prices to an index

  • Matched model. An item only contributes a price change if the same item was observed at the same store in both this week and the previous collection week. An item that reappears after a gap re-enters at its observed price and contributes no change — so a reappearance can never be read as inflation.
  • Jevons. Within a category, price changes are combined as a geometric mean.
  • Official weights. Categories are combined using the BLS CPI-U relative importances (), renormalized over the categories that have data that week.
  • Chaining and base. The index chains week to week; the base is the first full calendar month of collection. Until that month exists the base is provisional, and the index is not published at all until weeks of baseline exist.
  • Imputation and suppression. A missing pair carries its category's change forward and is counted; after four consecutive missing weeks it is dropped. If more than of a geography's basket is imputed, the cell is suppressed rather than published.

The time price

Hours to basket = weekly basket cost ÷ (average weekly wage ÷ 40). Wages are the QCEW average weekly wage for the county, all industries and ownerships — the same source for both series. Regions are employment-weighted from their counties. Between quarters the wage is interpolated; past the midpoint of the last published quarter it is held flat and flagged projected, because the next quarter will move it.

Coverage

A county is published when it has at least 3 stores across at least 2 banners. Below that it is thin coverage and rolls up to its region, or to the state. Counties are never interpolated to fill a map.

The regional reconstruction: “regional prices, local wages”

Store-level South Carolina prices from before collection started cannot be recovered. Retailer APIs return only today's price, and web archives do not hold store-specific prices. The only true historical source is commercial scanner data, which is paid and academic-only.

What we refuse to do: take today's measured basket and roll it backwards with a regional CPI. That produces a convincing chart and is exactly the thing this project exists to replace — a national number with a formula on top. It would look like measurement without being it.

What we do instead is build a separate series from published inputs only:

  • Prices: BLS average prices for the South Census region — actual dollars per pound, published monthly. Of the basket's items, have a like-for-like South series; the rest are excluded by name and reason (listed on the Basket page). The set is fixed for the whole history, so months are comparable.
  • Wages: QCEW, per county — the same measured local wages as above. This is the part of the number that is genuinely local, and it is also the part that varies most across South Carolina.
  • Gaps: BLS does not publish every item every month. A missing price moves with that same item's U.S. change; failing that, with the average change of the items BLS did publish for the South; failing that, the last price is carried for at most two months. Gaps are filled only from the past, so publishing a new month never changes an old one. The share of the basket that was imputed is published, and any month above is suppressed.

Treat it as an estimate of the historical pattern, not a measurement of South Carolina prices. The measured series supersedes it as it accumulates.

Quality gates

Nothing publishes unless every gate passes. A gate can only be bypassed by a human-recorded exception, and the exception is published with the data.

GateWhat it stops
unit_price_outlierA unit price more than 5× or less than 0.2× its own trailing 8-week median.
imputed_sharePublishing a geography whose basket is too heavily imputed.
banner_store_dropA silent collection failure: a banner's store count falling more than 20% in a week.
stratum_relativeA published category moving more than ±10% in one week.
unknown_price_typeMore than 10% of a week's prices being of unknown type.
week_has_observationsPublishing a week with no prices at all.
config_verifiedPublishing on unverified weights or a basket no human has signed off.
concepts_accountedAn item silently dropping out of the reconstruction without a stated reason.
wages_cover_historyA county missing a wage for a month other counties have.
no_silent_revisionAn already-published number changing under the same method version.

Revisions

Recomputing is free; changing a published number is not. Published snapshot files are never overwritten — a re-publication of the same period is written alongside as a new revision, and every file ever published stays listed with its checksum on the Data page. To change a published number the method version must change, and both versions stay downloadable.

One exception is declared in advance: hours computed on a projected wage are provisional by definition, and move when the next quarter of wage data is published.

What this does not tell you

  • One banner so far. Phase 0 collects from Kroger-family stores only, in one county. Other chains are deliberately not scraped pending a terms-of-service answer. Until more banners are in, the level is “what these stores charge,” not “what South Carolina charges.”
  • Stand-ins. Each item stands for a whole category. All seafood is priced as canned tuna; whole grains are priced with white bread, rice and pasta. That makes the basket cheaper than the diet USDA describes.
  • A nutritious diet, not a typical one. The Thrifty Food Plan is what a healthy budget diet costs. It holds more milk, produce and dried beans, and far less soda and snack food, than a typical cart.
  • Not the same as USDA's national figure. USDA prices the full category mix; we price the cheapest representative of each. A gap between the two numbers is mostly method, not geography.
